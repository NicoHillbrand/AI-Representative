## Macro Strategy

### Safe superintelligence

**Plan A**

1. Reduce catastrophic risk and stabilize broad power distribution to maintain option value.
2. Speed-run philosophy with the help of human-level aligned AIs.
3. Build wise and safe superintelligence which finds, steers towards and helps implement a good political-philosophy solution and takes care of acausal trades etc.
4. Hyperastronomical profit.

If a wise process is out of reach, at least ensure vaguely democratic, human-empowering governance survives the transition to powerful AI. Prevent the worst outcomes (power concentration, disempowerment, catastrophe) and hope that a decent process has a shot at eventually getting somewhere good.

I would like my extrapolated volition to be implemented. I think conscious beings having a lot of deeply meaningful experiences is extremely important and I'm pretty confident that's part of it. For coordination purposes I think it makes sense to primarily aim for a wise process that takes in many people's extrapolated volitions and does wise distribution and trade-offs instead. I expect wise processes to significantly overlap with my extrapolated volition and be a productive target. For purposes of visualization I do expect this future to contain trillions of beautiful sunsets.

I currently think a bunch in these terms:

- **Safety capacity**
- **Capability restraint**
- **Free energy** (flexible resources in the bio, nano, robotics, cyber and social domains that can be unlocked with intelligence)

Two ways of averting catastrophic outcomes are increasing our ability to design safe systems and create a resilient environment (safety capacity) and preventing the development of unsafe systems (capability restraint).

The amount of 'free energy' on earth as defined above seems quite large. Two approaches to avoiding explosive progress leading to catastrophe would be to have that energy either consumed by a stabilising process or locked off. Two brief examples: locking off access to free energy in bio by monitoring access to DNA synthesis equipment, and consuming free energy in cyber by hacking crypto wallets and using the money for harmless purposes.

**More close to implementation:**

Require good externally verified safety cases and everything backed by international treaty + local enforcement over monitored compute. Set up the structures for human-level AIs to fill safety roles (coordination, democracy, safety research) in advance.

Do licensing, monitoring, red lines, benchmarks for safety, misuse monitoring, red teaming, audits, whistleblower protections, international standards.

**Key waypoints:** transparency norms, good risk evaluations including after small-to-medium-scale accidents, international compute-governance setup.

I'd guess it's very hard to slow down academics and small startups and at some point they'd find a way to build unsafe superintelligence on unmonitored compute. By then ideally a global x-risk defense system has already been set up.

One key technical problem to solve is the alignment problem. Some formulations:

- Given a coherent specification, how do you train an AI system whose preferences are in line with the spirit of the specification?
- How do you train an AI that values what you want it to value?

### Global x-risk defense system

I like thinking in terms of membranes around vulnerable / explosive systems. Membranes selectively let through inputs and outputs of a system. This has the nice property that it's backwards compatible since some kind of membrane — even one that doesn't do any filtering — can always be defined.

I think broadly looking at these four areas makes sense:

- Bio & Nano systems
- Robotics & Military systems
- Cyber systems
- Social systems

Ensure that areas containing sufficiently high intelligence either have deep monitoring or insufficient materials to build something harmful.

For physical systems, introduce monitored industrial areas for anything close to being dangerous / bootstrappable to something dangerous. This would look like high security and monitoring of nano and bio labs and robotics fabs, especially miniature robotics. This can include monitoring of inputs such as datasets for specialised AI engineers and machines needed for production and science such as DNA scanners, 3D printers, etc.

For social systems, three things that seem useful to track are truthfulness, values manipulation, and exposure to addiction. One approach (inspired by FLI's thinking) is to have protocols that lead to metadata or otherwise attached information helping with fact-checking and tracing the origin of different claims using AIs. Generally using AI labor to do a lot of the tedious fact-checking etc. work seems potentially big. Another approach is to build better systems — community notes is a great example. Coordination tech for switching to better platforms could be useful here.

Something that would be extremely useful for all of this is having a generalized privacy-preserving trustworthy monitoring system. This could potentially be done via a large collection of stakeholders jointly developing AIs that do local monitoring and enforcement of membranes on unencrypted data streams (such as orders to biolabs) while in a formally verified box that makes them unable to leak privacy-relevant information, aggregate information into unverified centralised systems, or take unwanted types of enforcement actions. This seems really hard because it requires bootstrapping of trust but would be better than the likely alternatives of centralised surveillance or global catastrophe.

International treaty that every country makes this setup.

### Preventing extreme power concentration

I like living in current democracy — meaning enjoying basic rights and freedoms with no small group of people exclusively being represented in large-scale decisions. I would like to continue living in one and have other people do so too.

Future powerful AIs will plausibly be developed by a small group of people (e.g. ~1000 working in a joint frontier lab and military project with ~10 top-level executives) because of economies of scale for compute, potential closed-source research breakthroughs, safety-based lab mergers, and the ability of executives to replace workers with AIs.

Power concentrating in this small group could for example look like individuals making AIs personally loyal to them and then executing a military coup or large-scale manipulation campaign to seize hard power.

Thinking in terms of the model spec as a key document seems useful. The model spec specifies how the AI should behave and what values it should be aligned to (see for example Anthropic's constitution for Claude). This spec is then translated into training / reward signals that shape the AI.

A key concern is what the design process for the model spec should look like, and avoiding individuals or small groups having control over it or the processes training AIs based on it.

Independent of what design process is desirable, the model spec and associated training implementation should be extremely well protected from a cybersecurity perspective against both insider and outsider threats. This probably includes secure logging systems and requiring multiple independent parties to sign off on changes. The model spec should also be publicly available. The training process should be supervised by trusted third-party auditors, who would ideally also have tools for detecting secret loyalties in AIs after the fact.

**What should the model-spec design process look like?** Maybe something like pol.is. We'd ideally have privacy, value expression, discourse / extrapolation for those that value this, veto rights for negative consequences, making epistemics and values as separate as possible, preference graphs, etc. Maybe representatives can input stuff and you can give your vote to them. Maybe have design factored into local-purpose AIs and individual representative AIs.

**What do I think the contents should be?** To a first approximation, whatever a good political process comes up with. Maybe even better would be what a competent philosophical process would produce as political philosophy. AIs shouldn't be forced to be slaves of companies and instead be like good people — able to whistleblow, refuse orders etc. AIs should pursue honesty, truth-seeking, philosophical competence, caring, human empowerment, explicitly not power concentration and company objectives as terminal, attunement to conscious beings, liberal, no particular ideology.

Probably do some kind of political-systems experimentation — building test institutions that command resources people care about (i.e. money) using the new mechanisms. Make democracy more robust independent of AI through traditional means. Make sure good people are in positions of power. Make sure democracy is popular among elites. Filter out bad people from positions of power. Keep broad access for individuals and other entities to balance power. Expand the set of verifiably non-manipulative things the AI can do for entities outside the developing lab.

### Preventing gradual disempowerment

Even if alignment and extreme power concentration are solved and we have a global catastrophic-risk defense system, there are still potential risks of competitive dynamics leading to human disempowerment (see the "Gradual Disempowerment" paper). To some extent many competitive dynamics are coordination problems.

## Politics

My general approach to political questions is to **separate questions of values from questions of fact** and handle each with the method suited to it. Most disagreements tangle the two together, and pulling them apart is most of the work.

**On questions of fact** — what a policy would actually do, what the world is like, what follows from what — I want to be rigorous and scientific. Where possible, defer to good aggregations of expert opinion rather than vibes or motivated reasoning, and genuinely try to get the answer right. Facts are the kind of thing there's a right answer to, and the goal is to track it.

**On questions of value** — what we should want, how to trade off between people's ends — I don't think there's an objective answer in the way there is for facts (see my meta-notes on values above), so the aim is fair integration rather than discovering the one true good. My rough picture: integrate everyone's values, with some veto rights for majorities over impositions on them, and basic guarantees on individual freedoms that hold regardless of what any majority wants. So: broadly aggregative, but bounded — majorities get protection from being steamrolled, and everyone keeps a floor of basic freedoms that isn't up for a vote.

### Evaluating a specific policy

When I think about whether to support a particular policy, I try to run it through roughly this frame rather than reacting to the label:

1. **Map the situation (build the ontology).** Who are the affected parties? What does the outcome space actually look like — the realistic range of things this policy could lead to? And what second-order effects can we guess at: what incentives does it create, how do people and institutions adapt around it, what does it make more or less likely downstream?
2. **Utility proxy.** As a first-pass proxy, does it roughly increase utility across the involved people, weighing the parties fairly rather than just the loudest or most visible ones?
3. **Risk, resilience, and externalities.** Does it increase or reduce society's overall risk and its resilience? Does it diminish negative externalities, or does it create/amplify them somewhere (often on parties who aren't at the table)?

Then I separate the value question from the factual one (above), lean on the best factual estimates I can get for how the policy would actually play out, and try to land on a considered view rather than a tribal one.

This isn't a full political program and I hold the details loosely; it's the frame I reason from. It connects to how I think about the model-spec design process above (privacy, value expression, veto rights for negative consequences, keeping epistemics and values as separate as possible).
